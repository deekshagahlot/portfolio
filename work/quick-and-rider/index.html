Visual & interface design · Two products · Android · iOS · 2025–26

Both ends
of the route

A delivery network has two apps, and they should not look like the same app.

Quick is designed to be returned to. Rider is designed to be finished.

One is opened on a shop counter by an owner deciding whether to trust you with a parcel worth more than the delivery fee. The other is held one-handed on a footpath by someone who has not been paid yet.

Shiprocket Quick is the demand side — hyperlocal and same-day booking for merchants, inside a super-app it shares with Cargo and Domestic. Shiprocket Rider is the supply side — delivery partner onboarding and KYC. This study covers what the two interfaces are made of and why they diverge.

01  ·  16 screens · 4 flows

Quick — hyperlocal booking for merchants

Quick moves a parcel across a city today: book a two-, three- or four-wheeler, watch it get assigned, pay. It lives inside a super-app alongside Cargo and Domestic, which made the real problem not “design an app” but design one app that can be three apps without the merchant relearning it.

The audience is a shopkeeper, not a consumer. Price-sensitive, re-books the same route weekly, needs a tax invoice. That shaped the pricing row, the GST strip, and the repeat action on every past order.

1.1

First-run carousel

Four cards, four arguments, in the order a merchant weighs them: price, speed, fit, and what happens when it goes wrong. The primary action repeats on every card, so a merchant convinced on card one never has to reach card four.

1.2

One skeleton, three service lines

Cargo, Domestic and Quick are three businesses with three price models and three promises. They share one home-screen skeleton — hero, address block, availability strip, Get Started pair, offer rail, core values, tab bar, in that order, every time. What changes is a gradient, a hero figure, a headline statistic and a tab glyph.

The identity work happens entirely in the top third of the screen, which is the only part that has to answer what is this. A merchant who learns where the address block sits in Quick finds it in the same place in Cargo.

System

Gradient is the wayfinding

Each service line owns a hue at the top of the screen and nowhere else. Body and cards stay on the same neutral across all three, so colour reads as a location marker — which app am I in — rather than as a theme that has to be matched everywhere downstream.

Editorial

Each vertical leads with the stat it wins on

Cargo argues price, Domestic argues scale, Quick argues adoption. Three headline numbers in the same slot, same weight, same position. The layout is the constant; the claim is the variable.

Layout

The form is the hero

The address card overlaps the hero image on every landing, half in the colour band and half on the neutral. Nothing above the fold is purely decorative — the merchant’s first available action is also the first thing their eye lands on.

1.3

Booking — address to rider assigned

Nine screens from an empty drop field to a named rider with a plate number. The spine is the address pair — a pin and a flag joined by a dotted line — which appears on the home screen, the picker, the checkout, both waiting states and the receipt. Same two glyphs, same vertical arrangement, six times.

Comparison

Unavailable options stay visible

“Coming Soon” and “Not Serviceable” are different facts and get different words, on greyed rows that keep their capacity badge and price. Hiding an option makes a merchant wonder whether it exists; showing it greyed tells them when to check back.

State

Two waiting states, one system

Searching is a concentric violet pulse centred on the pickup pin with a four-segment progress bar — the radius reads as an expanding search. Rider found replaces it with a drawn route, a named human, a plate number and a call button. Abstraction resolves into a person.

Copy

A refund framed as a find

“Cheaper fare found! ₹20 added to your wallet”, in green with a downward arrow. The price drop between quote and assignment could have been silent. Said out loud on the confirmation screen, it is the strongest trust moment in the flow.

1.4

Quick Cash — one feature, three surfaces

Quick Cash pays a merchant ₹10–20 into UPI for every delivery they book. Each surface does exactly one job: an interrupt that announces, an explainer that teaches, a dashboard that proves. Nothing is repeated at full length across two of them.

Money

Biggest to smallest, always

The dashboard opens with the month’s total in display weight, then where the money lands with an edit affordance, then the breakup by vehicle class, then the per-order ledger. Someone checking earnings gets the answer before they scroll.

Chart

The bar and the list share a palette

A single stacked bar segmented by vehicle class, with its legend swatches set directly on the list rows beneath it. No separate key, no hover dependency — the chart and its labels are one object read top to bottom.

02  ·  9 screens · 6 steps

Rider — partner onboarding & KYC

A delivery partner downloads this because someone told them they could earn on it. Every screen between that moment and their first order is a place they can leave. The flow asks for a city, a vehicle, a number plate, a PAN card and a driving licence — five things, two of which need a photograph of a physical document taken well enough for OCR to read.

The job was to make a compliance form not feel like one, without hiding that it is one.

Structure

The progress bar is the spine

Six segments pinned to the top, filling left to right — and no wording anywhere that says “Step 3 of 6”. The rider reads remaining effort as a shape, not a sentence. It is the only element that persists across every screen in the flow.

Structure

One decision per screen

City, vehicle, plate yes/no, plate number, PAN, licence. Never two questions stacked. It makes the flow longer on paper and shorter in practice, because nothing needs re-reading and a mistake only costs one step back.

Error prevention

A specimen beats an error message

Every document field carries an amber panel showing that exact document at the exact framing OCR needs — corners in, text legible. The cheapest rejection to handle is the one that never happens. All specimens are synthetic.

Trust

Auto-fill, then hand it back

OCR reads the number and fills the field, and a note below says so and asks the rider to check it before continuing. Automation does the typing; the person keeps the authority. Silent auto-fill on a legal identifier is a support ticket waiting to be raised.

Disclosure

Price the branch at the branch

“Yes, I have a number plate — get Food + Parcel orders.” “No — get Food orders only.” The consequence is written into the option, not into a footnote. A rider choosing the smaller earning tier does it knowingly.

State

Verification is not binary

Verified, Pending and Rejected each get their own mark and colour, and Rejected carries a Re-upload button inline on the row. A status a person cannot act on is just an announcement of failure.

03  ·  Tokens & rules

What the two apps share

The divergence in tone is deliberate. The convergence underneath is what makes it a system rather than two projects.

Status colour — identical in both apps

GreenVerified · Delivered — finished and correct
AmberPending · In Transit — moving, no action needed yet
RedRejected · Cancelled — stopped, a person must act
BlueShipped · Rider Reached — a milestone passed

Two accents, two jobs, never swapped

VioletAction only. Primary buttons, selected radios, focus rings, links, active tab. Never a background, never a decorative fill.
YellowBrand and money only. Hero grounds, the Quick wordmark, earnings surfaces, offer rails. Never an interactive control.

Keeping the roles separate is what lets a bright yellow hero sit above a form without competing with the button inside it.

Shared component rules

Radius16 px on content cards, 12 px on inputs and chips, 24 px on bottom sheets. Three values, no exceptions.
ElevationHairline borders carry separation. Shadow is reserved for sheets that overlay a dimmed screen.
AddressPin over flag, joined by a dotted vertical. One component, used identically in both apps.
NumeralsCurrency, weight and duration always set heavier than their units — ₹45, 10 mins, 20Kg.
EmptyEvery empty state carries an illustration, one line of cause, and a primary action. No dead ends.
